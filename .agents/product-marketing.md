## Product Overview
**One-liner:** High-fiber snack bites made from upcycled fruit pulp, built taste first by a Michelin-trained chef.
**What it does:** One serving is five 10 g bites (50 g) with 16 g of fiber, about 57% of the daily value. Three flavors. It is a snack people want to eat that happens to close part of the fiber gap; it is never sold as a supplement, protocol or result.
**Product category:** High-fiber snack; sits near protein bars, energy bites and better-for-you snacks. People search "high fiber snack", "fiber snacks", "healthy office snacks".
**Product type:** D2C e-commerce plus direct B2B sales to offices.
**Business model:** Single servings online at $4.99; bigger orders, office boxes and monthly subscriptions get lower per-pouch prices. Suggested retail $4.49 per pouch. Pre-orders charged at time of order, no promised ship date. Store on Shopify at shop.zucasnacks.com (password-protected until launch).
**Flavors:** Peanut Cookie Dough, Chocolate Raspberry Sea Salt, Maple Pecan (not "Maple Butter Pecan"). Office boxes mix all three. [NEED: confirm the peanut flavor name; one Nov 13 note says Peanut Chocolate Chip.]

## Target Audience
**Target companies:** Bay Area startups and tech offices that stock snacks (the larger segment; lead with businesses before individuals).
**Decision-makers:** Office managers, workplace or people-ops leads, founders at small startups.
**Consumers:** People in their 20s and 30s who care what is in their food and want something that tastes good, not a health food.
**Primary use case:** A snack you reach for anyway that carries real fiber.
**Jobs to be done:**
- Give the team a snack that is better than the usual pantry without anyone feeling lectured.
- Get more fiber in without supplements or a chalky bar.
- Buy food whose story they can repeat: made from fruit pulp that would have been thrown away.
**Use cases:**
- Office pantry restock, ordered as a box sized to headcount (default one pouch per employee per weekday).
- Afternoon snack at a desk or after training.
- Monthly subscription for a household.

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** Most people fall short on fiber, and the convenient options either taste like health food or get their fiber from isolated added fibers chosen because they are easy to formulate with.
**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Protein bars solve a problem most people do not have; almost no one falls short on protein.
- Fiber bars and supplements put the tradeoff in the taste or in an ingredient list nobody can read.
- Office snack boxes optimize for variety, not for what is in the food.
**What it costs them:** Snacks bought and left uneaten; or eating something they do not enjoy out of obligation.
**Emotional tension:** Wanting to eat better without feeling preached at or giving up taste.

## Competitive Landscape
Praise others by name; criticize behavior, never a named brand's taste. No head-to-head where Zuca wins.
**Direct:** High-fiber snacks and fiber-forward bars. Fall short because the fiber is often isolated and chosen for convenience, not for the eater.
**Secondary:** Protein bars and energy bites (e.g. Barebells, protein bites in pillow pouches). Taste well; tradeoff moves to the ingredient list, and they solve protein, not fiber.
**Indirect:** Fiber supplements, fruit, office snack subscription boxes.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- 16 g of fiber per 50 g serving in a snack built taste first.
- Made from upcycled fruit pulp (pomace), which is hard to formulate with; Zuca took the harder road on purpose.
- Made by a chef: Michelin training, national cooking competition winner, Stanford bioengineering.
**How we do it differently:** Chef-built flavors on a fiber base most brands avoid because it is difficult to bind.
**Why that's better:** The fiber comes with the fruit's own story, and the eater does not trade taste for it.
**Why customers choose us:** Taste, then the story, then the number.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Made from apple pulp, is that gross or safe?" | It's the fruit. Pressing puts the juice in a bottle; the pulp is the same apple, concentrated in fiber. |
| "Isn't this another protein bar?" | No. Almost no one is short on protein; most people are short on fiber. |
| "16 g of fiber is a lot." | New eaters can start with one bite. [Register C13 is Exploring; confirm before publishing.] |
| "Can I eat it with my diet?" | Point to the per-flavor allergen statement and the pack panel. Never vegan, never gluten-free. |

**Anti-persona:** Anyone avoiding gluten (oats are not certified gluten-free and the facility handles wheat); vegans (two flavors contain ghee); anyone looking for a supplement, weight-loss product or medical outcome.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** Pantry snacks go uneaten or feel like junk; fiber products taste like obligation.
**Pull:** Tastes like dessert, 16 g of fiber, a founder and story they can repeat.
**Habit:** Existing snack-box vendor, familiar protein bars.
**Anxiety:** Will people eat it, is upcycled food safe, is 16 g too much fiber, will it arrive.

## Customer Language
**How they describe us (taster quotes on the site, unattributed):**
- "Can't. Stop. Eating them!!!"
- "These are dangerously addictive."
- "I can't believe this is upcycled fruit."
- "How has no one thought of this before?"
- "I can't believe this is made from food that otherwise would have been thrown away."
**Words to use (Emil's):** upcycled, diverted, overlooked, transparent, back to basics, the flavors are true, food is medicine (story only, never a product promise), a tasty brand that happens to be super good for you.
**Words to avoid:** delicious, amazing, incredible, a treat, enjoyable (evaluative food words); clinically proven, physician-recommended, doctor-approved; zero waste, carbon negative; vegan, plant-based, gluten-free; no refined sugar, no added sugar; supplement, protocol, results; "sold out" or "pre-ordered" for anything that was not a sale.

## Brand Voice
Full rules: `claude/Zuca_Voice_Profile.md`. Summary:
| Attribute | Means | Doesn't mean | Example line |
|-----------|-------|--------------|--------------|
| Blunt and honest | Straight to the point, respects the reader's time | Superior, snarky, contrarian | "Almost no one falls short on protein." |
| Translator | Plain version first, mechanism on top | Expert asserting authority | "It's the fruit." |
| Sensory | Describe food by what you feel and taste | Evaluating it | "Chocolate Raspberry Sea Salt is tart and deep, with the salt landing at the end." |
| Gaps first | Names Zuca's own gaps before anyone asks | Hedging what is known | |

**Tone by context:**
- Support: warm, personal, invites a reply; signed "With gratitude".
- Launch: plain facts, no countdowns, no fake scarcity, no guilt or urgency.
- Errors: own it ("That's our end, not yours").
- Sales (offices): numbers and logistics first, character intact.
- Social: follow the journey, three segments, brand late.
**Never use (and why):** em dashes (house rule); "it's not X, it's Y" (AI tell); urgency or guilt (Emil's rule, even when it converts better); sustainability as the lead (it is a trait, not a selling point).
